# Design schemas and evidence

Based on the supplied course deck, `2 - Rules of Play.pptx`. These are implementation interpretations, not claims that player learning has been proven.

## Games as Systems of Information (course slides 86–95)

The HUD presents the next job; the notebook gives the shared core's current location and discovered drawing; machine prompts distinguish prerequisite, power, routing and completion states. Routing boards show reachable power, required contact counts and outlet counts. Hush's current activity is visible. Knowledge of dependencies and patrol timing affects decisions. The drawing's recipient is narrative information revealed before the final practical task.

Evidence: FactoryGame, Machine, CircuitView, Inspector. During player tests, ask players to explain why a route remains open after power is removed, and why a board with a lit outlet can still be incomplete. Ambiguous feedback should be revised rather than treated as desirable mystery.

## Games as Systems of Uncertainty (course slides 68–84)

Uncertainty concerns solving unfamiliar circuits and choosing a safe time to move around a predictable inspector. Circuit topology, machine prerequisites and patrol rules are deterministic. Rotations, observation and retries reduce uncertainty through knowledge. Opening the routing panel pauses the world so conceptual planning does not require fast input.

Evidence: CircuitGrid, Inspector, FactoryGame.ResetSection. Observe whether players deliberately use sight-blocking crates or machine investigation windows. Existing automated checks establish rules, not the subjective experience of uncertainty.

## Emergent Systems (course slides 46–67): conditional claim

Crate occlusion, a movable shared core, retained manufacturing progress and machine noise can combine into different local plans. Do not claim rich emergence until playtests record distinct successful strategies. The present factory progression is deliberately authored and ordered.

Do not label ordinary success sounds as cybernetic balancing feedback. They communicate outcomes but are not evidence of an implemented negative-feedback balance loop.
